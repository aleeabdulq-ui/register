1. Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive data analysis of the IT Asset Register for The Building Practice Limited (TBP) as of 8 August 2026. The register captures 49 IT assets across 15 categories, representing a total capital investment of ₦43,191,000 with an additional ₦26,240,000 in software licence renewal exposure.

Key Finding: Over half (53%) of all IT assets have expired warranties, representing significant financial risk. Architecture department holds 63% of total asset value, underscoring the firm's heavy reliance on technology for design deliverables.

The analysis reveals that the organisation has made substantial capital investments in IT infrastructure between 2022 and 2025, with peak expenditure of ₦13.1M in 2024. However, several critical areas require immediate management attention: 26 assets operating without warranty coverage, 4 expired software licences, 8 overdue maintenance tasks, and 4 assets that could not be physically located during verification.

2. Scope & Methodology

Data Sources

This analysis draws from the following data sources within the TBP IT Asset Register workbook:

  • IT Asset Register: Primary inventory of 49 assets with 39 data fields per asset
  • LP Audit (March 2023): Historical laptop audit data used as baseline reconciliation
  • LP Cost Records: Procurement cost data for 25 laptop/desktop purchases
  • Maintenance Schedule: 49 maintenance records tracking service history
  • Software & Licences: 20 software licence records across the organisation
  • Asset Verification: Physical verification results for all 49 assets

Methodology

The analysis employed quantitative and qualitative methods including descriptive statistics, financial aggregation, ageing analysis, trend analysis of capital expenditure, compliance assessment against warranty and licence terms, and risk scoring based on asset condition and coverage gaps. All monetary values are denominated in Nigerian Naira (₦/NGN).

4.3 Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Estimate

Beyond the initial capital outlay of ₦43.2M, the ongoing operational costs are substantial:

  • Software Licence Renewals: ₦26,240,000 annually (60.7% of initial CapEx)
  • Maintenance Costs: ₦2,455,000 per cycle (5.7% of initial CapEx)

9.1 Missing Asset Analysis

Four assets (8.2%) could not be physically located during verification. This represents a potential loss exposure depending on the value of the missing items. Investigation should determine whether these assets were:

  • Moved to an unregistered location
  • Assigned to remote/off-site personnel without updating records
  • Stolen or lost
  • Disposed of without following the retirement process

Recommendation: A secondary sweep should be conducted within 14 days, including contact with all custodians. Any assets still unaccounted for should be formally reported and insurance claims initiated where applicable.

11. Recommendations

Immediate Actions (0–30 days)

1

Renew All Expired Software Licences Critical

Immediately renew the 4 expired licences to restore compliance and eliminate legal/security exposure. Estimated cost: ~₦1.5M based on portfolio averages.

2

Investigate & Resolve Missing Assets Critical

Conduct a secondary physical sweep within 14 days for the 4 unlocated assets. Escalate to security if unresolved. Initiate insurance claims where applicable.

3

Clear Overdue Maintenance Backlog High

Schedule and complete the 8 overdue maintenance tasks immediately. Prioritise servers and network equipment to prevent service disruption.

4

Patch OS Updates on 7 Non-Compliant Devices High

Deploy Windows updates on all 7 flagged computing assets within 7 days to close cybersecurity vulnerabilities.

Short-Term Actions (30–90 days)

5

Evaluate Extended Warranty Programme Medium

For the 26 assets with expired warranties, evaluate cost-benefit of purchasing extended warranties (e.g., HP Care Pack, Dell ProSupport, Lenovo Premier). For assets >4 years old, consider replacement instead.

6

Autodesk Licence Optimisation Medium

Audit actual usage of Autodesk products (₦11.9M/year). Evaluate whether all 15 AutoCAD and 10 Revit seats are actively used. Consider downgrading unused seats to viewer licences or switching to Flex token model.

7

Establish 3-Year Refresh Cycle Medium

Formalise a hardware refresh policy: laptops every 3–4 years, desktops every 4–5 years, servers every 5 years. Budget ₦8–12M annually for cyclical replacements.

Long-Term Strategic Actions (90+ days)

8

Vendor Consolidation Strategy Low

Reduce the 20-brand landscape to 5–7 preferred vendors. Standardise on HP/Dell for computing, Cisco/Ubiquiti for networking. Negotiate enterprise agreements for better pricing and SLAs.

9

Implement Automated Asset Tracking Low

Deploy an IT asset management platform (e.g., Snipe-IT, ManageEngine, or Lansweeper) with automated discovery, licence tracking, and alert workflows to replace the manual spreadsheet-based process.

10

Develop IT Asset Disposal Policy Low

Create a formal asset disposal/retirement procedure covering data sanitisation (NIST 800-88), environmental compliance, and documentation requirements for the Disposal/Retirement fields currently blank in the register.

12. Conclusion

The TBP IT Asset Register provides a solid foundation for technology governance, capturing comprehensive data across 49 assets, 20 software licences, and full maintenance and verification records. The total IT investment of ₦43.2M in hardware and ₦26.2M in annual software renewals represents a significant commitment to technology-enabled service delivery.

However, the analysis reveals several critical governance gaps that require immediate attention: the 53% warranty expiry rate creates substantial unplanned cost exposure, 4 expired software licences pose compliance risks, and 4 missing assets indicate weaknesses in physical custody controls.

The recommendations in this report, if implemented systematically, will strengthen IT governance, reduce financial risk, improve cybersecurity posture, and establish a sustainable lifecycle management framework. The estimated cost of inaction—factoring in potential hardware failures without warranty, licence penalties, and security incidents—could exceed ₦5M in the next 12 months alone.

Bottom Line: Proactive investment of ~₦3–5M in warranty extensions, licence renewals, and process improvements will protect the firm's ₦43.2M technology investment and prevent significantly larger unplanned expenditures.
